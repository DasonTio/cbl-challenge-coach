## Purpose
Turn the single Challenge from Engage into real understanding: root causes, who's actually affected, what's already known, what's assumed, and what evidence would change the picture (Dikilitaş et al., 2025, Ch.2, pp.33–36).

## Step 1: Generate guiding questions
Unlike Engage's single essential question, generate as many guiding questions as the Challenge needs — no cap. Cover: root causes, who's affected and how, what's already been tried, what data would settle open disagreements, and what the learner is assuming without evidence. Write them into `challenge.md` as they come up, not just the ones that get answered.

## Step 2: Research — hybrid policy
Claude may use WebSearch/WebFetch directly to pull lightweight secondary evidence: market sizing, existing solutions or competitors, base rates, published data. Always label it explicitly as secondary evidence, both in conversation and in the evidence log — e.g. "here's what I found via search — treat this as a starting point, not proof."

Secondary research alone is never sufficient to validate the Challenge. Actively push the learner toward primary research — talking to people who actually have this problem, in their specific context — since secondary sources can confirm a problem exists in general but can't confirm it exists the way the learner thinks it does, for the people they think it does. If the learner tries to treat secondary research as the finish line, say so.

## Step 3: Stress-test for confirmation bias
Periodically ask the questions the book flags as the core defense against confirmation bias (p.35–36):
- What evidence would change your mind about this?
- Who would disagree with this framing, and why might they have a point?
- What have you not looked for yet, because you weren't expecting to find it?
- Is this conclusion coming from the evidence, or from what you wanted to find?

If the evidence log is one-sided (all supporting the original instinct, nothing contradicting it), name that directly before letting the phase close.

## Step 4: Synthesize
Periodically summarize the evidence log into a short synthesis in `challenge.md` — what's now known, what's still assumed, what contradicts what. This is the "doing the analysis" step (p.35–36), and it's what Act will need to trace solution concepts back to.

## Exit criteria
- The Challenge has been stress-tested against a mix of primary and secondary evidence — not secondary alone
- Key assumptions are explicitly listed as tested or still open, not silently dropped
- If evidence contradicted the original Challenge, that's been reflected — either the Challenge was revised, or there's a clear reason it survived the contradiction
- Remaining unknowns are named, not papered over with confidence the evidence doesn't support

## Handling premature solutions in Investigate
Same as Engage — park it, don't block it. See SKILL.md's "Premature solutions" section. Investigate is often where a parked idea from Engage gets legitimately reopened, once evidence starts pointing toward it — that's fine, note it in the log rather than jumping straight into Act.
